03 Analytical readout

Three signals worth carrying forward.

The useful output is the next operations question, not a false sense of certainty.

01 / TIMING

Hour-of-day is the real demand lever.

Demand ranges from about 1,320 bookings at 4 AM to about 12,400 at 6 PM. The shape repeats across weekday and weekend periods.

02 / FAILURE

Raw failure counts would mislead.

Busy hours naturally create more failed bookings. The documented rate stays around 24–26% instead of tracking the demand curve.

03 / CAVEAT

The dataset needs an honest label.

Flat day-of-week behavior, broad pickup geography, and tight fare/distance bands suggest a synthetic or simulated dataset.

OPS RECOMMENDATION

Fix the matching system, not just the rush hour.

The data supports a change in the next question an operations team should ask.

  1. Measure matching failure by segment: service type, pickup zone, driver availability, and cancellation reason.
  2. Use hourly demand for capacity planning, not as proof that peak volume causes service failure.
  3. Validate against organic dispatch logs before translating the finding into a production intervention.

04 Method & source

Show the work.

Transparent method is part of the portfolio, especially when the dataset has limits.

DATASET

150,000 NCR ride bookings.

One year of timestamps, booking status, pickup/drop locations, fare, distance, and cancellation reasons.

APPROACH

EDA + rate diagnostics.

Parsed hour, day-of-week, and month fields; compared demand, geography, and service health using rates rather than raw counts.

LIMITATION

Likely simulated data.

The repository flags unusually flat weekday behavior, geographic spread, and fare/distance bands. This is an analytical exercise, not an organic Uber benchmark.
